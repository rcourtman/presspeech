Presspeech vs Superscribe Lite

Both offer no-subscription, local dictation for Mac and Windows. Superscribe Lite pastes when you stop by default, has a second shortcut that types words while you speak, and offers optional cleanup through a provider you configure. Presspeech transcribes after recording, checks the original paste destination, and keeps a manual recovery path when it cannot verify that destination. These are workflow tradeoffs, not measured claims about accuracy or reliability.

Presspeech and Superscribe Lite compared
Question Presspeech Superscribe Lite
Dictation workflow Hold a hotkey or use toggle mode, speak, then transcribe after stopping. It normally pastes only after confirming the same window is still focused; otherwise it leaves the complete transcript for manual paste. Hold a shortcut or tap it for hands-free recording. Mac defaults to Option+Space and Windows to F9, which paste when you stop; Option+Shift+Space or Shift+F9 types the words while you speak.
Recognition Local recognition on each platform: multilingual Parakeet TDT v3 on macOS, with opt-in Parakeet Ultra from 0.3.10; Windows defaults to multilingual Parakeet when usable NVIDIA CUDA is available, otherwise English-only Whisper base.en. Other Windows models are selectable. Choose a local speech model for the language you speak; the vendor's Lite page lists 16 languages, with models from 29 MB to 339 MB, and says Lite does not detect the language by itself.
Text handling Deterministic dictionary corrections, shortcuts, and filler removal on both platforms; macOS also has opt-in spoken formatting. There is no generative rewrite or provider-cleanup path. Local recognition is the default. Optional cleanup can remove filler words, change punctuation, or rewrite text using an API key the user supplies to a provider; the cleanup request sends text to that provider.
Privacy and network No account or cloud transcription path. Model downloads and optional release checks use the network, and bundled-library behavior can differ by release. Review the version-specific network inventory and platform-specific macOS and Windows notices before choosing a build. The vendor says Lite has no account or sign-in and keeps audio and transcripts on the computer when optional cleanup is not used. Models download once per language from Superscribe's server (Hugging Face for six languages), and the Mac app makes small settings requests to superscribe.io that the vendor says carry nothing dictated; optional cleanup sends text to the provider configured by the user.
Platforms and release status At the 30 October 2026 check, the Mac release was 0.3.10 (signed and notarised for Apple Silicon); the Windows build was 0.1.12, an unsigned x64 prerelease. The vendor offers separate downloads for Apple silicon Macs on macOS 13 or later and for Windows 10 or 11 on x64 or ARM64, and says the Mac app is signed and notarized and the Windows installer is signed. Its September 4, 2026 launch post identified version 0.3.1; the current Lite page states no version.
Published performance evidence Publishes model-only p50 latency on four synthetic Mac clips; microphone capture, transcription startup, and paste are outside that measurement. No directly comparable latency or accuracy benchmark was identified in the reviewed public materials. This does not establish a speed or accuracy winner.
Price Free and MIT licensed. Free; no account or subscription is required for Lite, according to the vendor.

Choose Superscribe Lite if you want to see text arrive during speech, select a local model for your language, or optionally clean up text with a provider you configure. Choose Presspeech if you prefer a fixed, after-recording workflow, deterministic text handling, and explicit same-window checks with clipboard recovery when automatic paste is unsafe. Test either app in the target fields and languages you depend on; these public materials do not provide a controlled comparison of recognition or delivery quality.

Sources: Presspeech's source and platform documentation, version-specific privacy inventory, benchmark methodology, and target-app protocol; Superscribe's Lite page, September 4, 2026 Lite launch note, and official site (checked 30 October 2026). Superscribe feature and privacy statements above are the vendor's claims, not independent verification. No cross-product latency or accuracy winner is inferred.
